# A Global Analysis of the Digital Divide (1990–2021)

## 1. Project Description
This project analyses how internet access has changed across more than 200 countries from 1990 to 2021. It looks at three dimensions of connectivity: the percentage of people using the internet, the total number of internet users, and the growth of mobile phone subscriptions to tell the story of who got connected, who did not, and why.
The goal is to uncover clear patterns in global digital inequality, understand the role that mobile technology played in bringing connectivity to developing nations, and place Kenya and East Africa within that global picture. The analysis combines data cleaning, feature engineering, statistical analysis, and visualisation to produce the complete analysis.


## 2. Problem Statement
The internet has become one of the most important tools for education, economic opportunity, healthcare, and political participation in the modern world. The gap between countries that are fully connected and those that are not is known but without a clear data driven picture of where this gap exists and how it has changed over time, it is difficult to make the case for investment, policy change, or targeted intervention. This project aims to provide that picture, showing not just where the world stands today, but how we got here and how much progress has actually been made.
